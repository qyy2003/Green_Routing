Green Routing · per-link forecasting · experiments of 1 Oct 2026

Month Window and Smoothing

A 30-day input window makes day-ahead and week-ahead forecasts 2.6–4.7% more accurate on the typical link for four of the five models, and does nothing for 15-minute and 1-hour forecasts. Smoothing the forecast path with a moving average of up to one hour makes 8-hour-to-1-week forecasts 2–4× less jagged for under 0.3% extra error. Retraining the models on smoothed traffic costs more accuracy for the same smoothness.

How the experiments were run

Each experiment changes one thing against the reproduced year-scale baseline and keeps the rest: the data (2025-07-01 to 2026-07-11, 534 links with mean ≥ 1 Mbps), the train / validation / test split, the calendar features, the asymmetric peak-aware loss, up to 100 epochs with early stopping, and the per-model seeds. Changing the window length also changes three things that would otherwise confound it, so those are controlled explicitly.

Exp 1 · month windowInput window 7 → 30 days (2,016 → 8,640 five-minute steps) for GRU, TCN, DLinear and PatchTST. Controls: batch pinned at the baseline's 85 (the code would otherwise shrink it to 32 for long windows); TCN depth uncapped from 9 to 12 levels so its receptive field (16,381 steps) actually covers 30 days; PatchTST patches 16/8 → 64/32 so the transformer sees 269 tokens instead of 1,079, about the baseline's 251. GBDT has no input window, so its month variant adds weekly lags (1–4 weeks back) and 1-week and 30-day rolling features.
TCN budgetA month TCN step costs about 9× a week step, too slow to train to convergence (the baseline TCN ran 71 epochs). Both TCNs in this comparison were trained for the same 20 epochs, so the comparison is fair, but neither is a converged model.
Exp 2a · post-smoothingThe baseline models' forecast paths are smoothed with a centred moving average of 15 min, 1 h or 3 h. Every value used comes from the same forecast origin, so no information from later origins leaks in.
Exp 2b · smoothed targetThe four neural models are retrained on a 1-hour centred moving average of each link's traffic instead of the raw 5-minute series. Inputs stay raw. Scoring is always against the raw 5-minute traffic.
ScoringEach fitted model is re-run over the whole 111-day test period. Block mode forecasts each horizon window by window: every 5-minute step gets a forecast, 31,470 targets per link. Rolling mode scores the forecast made exactly h earlier, for every 15-minute origin: about 10,400 targets per link. All models in a mode are scored on the same targets. A comparison is paired per link: the change in MAE on that link, the share of links that improve, and a Wilcoxon signed-rank test across links.

Experiment 1 · a month of input

Longer horizons gain, short ones don't. At 1 day ahead, the month window lowers the typical link's error by 3.4% for DLinear, 2.8% for PatchTST, 2.6% for GBDT and 4.7% for the 20-epoch TCN, each on 78–90% of links. At 1 week ahead the gains are 1.2–3.2%. At 15 minutes and 1 hour ahead the changes are within ±1% for most models, and the TCN gets clearly worse (+4.8% and +3.1%). A month of history helps with what happens next week, and adds nothing about the next hour.

GRU is the exception. Its change at 1 day is −0.1% (not significant) and at 1 week +0.9%. A recurrent network has to carry 8,640 steps of state to use a month of history, and it doesn't. The other models reach the old weeks directly: through convolutions (TCN), a linear map (DLinear), attention over patches (PatchTST) or explicit lag features (GBDT).

The leaderboard moves at the bill's horizons. In rolling mode, the best day-ahead median error ratio against persistence goes from 0.749 to 0.733 (GBDT with month features). At 8 hours it goes from 0.487 to 0.477 (PatchTST) and at 1 week from 0.856 to 0.830 (GBDT). In block mode the fully trained week TCN stays the best 8-hour forecaster (0.659). The month PatchTST takes 1 day (0.671 → 0.662), and the month DLinear takes 1 week (0.674 → 0.671).

Which links gain

The gain grows with how weekly a link's traffic is. Splitting links into thirds by the autocorrelation of their traffic one week apart, the 1-day gain goes from −1.6% to −5.3% for DLinear, −1.8% to −3.8% for PatchTST and −1.6% to −3.9% for GBDT. Level drift over the training period doesn't explain it. A month window holds four copies of the weekly cycle, and links that repeat week after week benefit from the averaging.

When it gains

Most of the gain lands on weekends. On Saturdays and Sundays the month window lowers PatchTST's day-ahead pooled WAPE by 2.5 and 2.9 points on average, while weekdays average within ±1 point; over the 110 days it is better on 57. Holidays split: Labour Day, Ascension and Corpus Christi improve by about 3 points, while Good Friday and Easter Monday get 5–9 points worse.

What it costs

Step time grows with the window for every model except PatchTST, whose token count was held constant. The month TCN's 9× step cost is why it could only be trained for a 20-epoch budget here.

Experiment 2 · smooth instead of spiky

The forecasts are already much smoother than the traffic. Roughness here is the mean step-to-step change of the forecast divided by that of the actual traffic. For 8-hour block forecasts it is 0.16 for GRU, 0.18 for TCN, 0.26 for PatchTST and 0.45 for DLinear. DLinear is the jaggedest because its linear seasonal map copies past spikes forward. Most of the spikiness in the plots is the traffic itself, plus a jump at each window start where a new forecast begins.

Up to an hour of smoothing is nearly free at the horizons the bill uses. A 1-hour moving average cuts roughness by 2–4× (TCN 0.18 → 0.09, DLinear 0.45 → 0.12). At 8 hours to 1 week ahead it changes the typical link's MAE by −0.2% to +0.3%. A 15-minute average costs at most 0.2% at any horizon of an hour or more. Peaks are shaved slightly more (TCN 8 h peak bias −23.4% → −23.9%), and hourly- and daily-volume errors don't move (TCN 8 h: 22.7% → 22.8% and 11.1% → 11.1%).

Wider windows and short horizons are where smoothing hurts. A 3-hour average costs up to 1.9% at 8 hours and 1 day (0.9% for TCN, nothing for GRU). At 15 minutes ahead, a 1-hour average costs 2.3–7.9% and a 3-hour one 12–22%, because the near-term forecast's job is to track the current level, which an average blurs.

Retraining on a smoothed target is the worse way to get the same smoothness. The retrained models come out about as smooth as a 1-hour post-average (roughness 0.09–0.16), but at 8 hours they lose 2.5% (TCN), 3.2% (PatchTST), 8.0% (DLinear) and 1.5% (GRU) of per-link accuracy. Only GRU at 1 day ahead improved (−1.5%, 63% of links), together with its pooled WAPE (32.9% → 31.7%). A model trained on raw targets already learns where the traffic level is going; averaging its output afterwards costs little.

What to change

  1. Use a 1-month window for day-ahead and week-ahead forecasting with GBDT (month lag features), PatchTST (patch 64/32) or DLinear: 2.6–3.4% lower per-link error at 1 day, on about 80% of links, at 1.3× (PatchTST) and 4× (DLinear) the training-step cost. Keep the 1-week window for 15-minute and 1-hour forecasts.
  2. Don't extend GRU: it doesn't use the extra history. Decide on TCN after a full-length run: at equal 20-epoch budgets the month TCN is the biggest day-ahead gainer (−4.7%), but at 9× the step cost it needs a GPU budget the others don't.
  3. If a smooth forecast is wanted, post-smooth it: a centred moving average of 15 minutes to 1 hour on each forecast path, for horizons of 8 hours and longer. Leave 15-minute and 1-hour forecasts raw.
  4. Don't retrain on smoothed targets, with GRU at 1 day as the one case worth a second look.
  5. For carbon accounting, smoothness is safe. Hourly- and daily-volume errors are almost unchanged by up-to-1-hour smoothing, so a smooth forecast bills the same as a spiky one.

Limits of these results

  • One seed per model. The baseline report measured run-to-run noise at about ±1% on the per-link median. The headline gains (2.6–4.7% at 1 day, 78–90% of links, p p < 10−20lt; 10−35) are well above it. Changes under 1% are not.
  • TCN month is a matched-budget comparison. The 20-epoch week TCN is 3–4% worse than the converged one at 8 hours and 1 day, so the month TCN at 20 epochs only roughly ties the converged week TCN at 1 day.
  • PatchTST's month variant also changes patch length (16 → 64 steps), to keep the token count. The comparison is month input at a constant transformer budget, which is not quite month input alone.
  • Month models need a gap-free 30-day history. They could forecast 30 fewer of 50,472 sampled points (0.06%). Common-target scoring drops those points for every model.
  • Validation losses are not comparable across windows, because each window is normalised by its own mean and spread. The comparisons here use test error in Mbps on identical targets.
  • Experiment logs ran with TF32 matrix maths for speed (about 3–7 × 10−4 relative difference from full float32, measured against each run's own records), and rolling mode scores every 15th minute rather than every 5th. Both are far below the effects reported.

Data and how to reproduce

Everything is in the Green Routing repository and under its artifacts/ directory. Per-link, per-model, per-horizon metrics are in the CSVs. The full prediction logs can be browsed link by link in the prediction viewer (gr-viewer, port 8138), where the variants appear as models such as tcn@month, tcn+ma1h and tcn@starget.
