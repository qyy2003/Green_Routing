Trained models on 24 weeks of 5-minute history

Trained models can read 24 weeks of 5-minute values (48,384 steps per link). The ladder's models cannot because their cost grows with every input step. Patches, multi-resolution tokens, folding by week or weekly profiles make 24 weeks cost 4 to 370 ms per training step. With 5-minute input and output kept, going from 1 to 4 weeks of context lowers day-ahead error by about 1.5 points and week-ahead error by about 2 to 2.5. Going further to 24 weeks adds nothing, for every method tried, across three seeds. Timestral, given the same 5-minute input, still leads by 1.0 (I2) to 1.7 points (Switch LAN) at a day.

Error against context length

All trained models here take 5-minute values in and give a 5-minute path out. Each point is a full test-block score. The trained curve falls from 1 to 4 weeks and is flat after that. The transformer that reads every hour of 24 weeks directly is worse than the same model at 4 weeks.

How each model reads 24 weeks

The ladder's models scale with the input length. DLinear needs a weight per input step and horizon, which is 97 million at 24 weeks. The GRU steps through 48,384 values one by one, the TCN needs another dilation level for every doubling, and PatchTST flattens every token into its output head. The models below keep the input at 5 minutes and cap the cost. All are global models trained on every link, with inputs scaled by the mean of the last week and an L1 loss on the 5-minute targets.

A scaling problem that grows with context

Each window is divided by the mean of its last week. That keeps the recent level near 1, but old weeks are unbounded. On a link that is nearly idle now and was busy months ago, old values reach millions. The longer the context, the more windows contain such weeks. The profile MLP, which has no normalisation layer, diverged: its training loss went into the thousands and it scored 27.5 % on I2 at 24 weeks.

Bounding the input fixes this. Values up to 10 times the recent mean pass unchanged; above that, the input grows only with the logarithm (Timestral uses a similar squash). The profile MLP at 24 weeks drops from 27.48 to 18.63 % (I2) and from 36.76 to 32.42 % (Switch LAN). The transformers, whose layer norms already absorbed most of it, move by about half a point or less.

Why the gain stops at four weeks

  • Plain weekly profiles stop improving at 4 weeks too. The per-5-minute-of-week median over K weeks, rescaled to the last week's level, scores 22.43 / 22.41 / 22.59 % on I2 for K = 4 / 12 / 24. On Switch LAN it worsens: 37.47 / 39.18 / 41.65 %, because links change level and shape over months. The extra weeks hold little that simple statistics can use.
  • Timestral gains little beyond 4 weeks at 5 minutes, too. 16 weeks of 5-minute input vs 4 weeks: I2 16.48 vs 16.77 %, −0.29 [−0.71, +0.11], not significant; Switch LAN 29.07 vs 29.79 %, −0.72 [−1.29, −0.25] at a day and +0.26 (not significant) at a week. With hourly input its gain from 4 to 24 weeks is 0.75 (I2) and 1.44 points (Switch LAN). That gain comes from a prior learned on a large corpus. A model trained on 113 to 534 links and one to two years does not have it.
  • Validation said 24 weeks would help; the test said no. On the validation block, 24-week runs tie or beat 4-week ones (I2 24.03 to 24.72 vs 24.48 to 24.80). On the later test block they do not, which suggests that what the extra weeks teach is specific to the training period.
  • Neither fix helps. Summarising old weeks as hourly means gives 18.54 / 31.94 %. That removes the gap between I2's hourly-filled training history and its real 5-minute test history. Training on randomly truncated contexts gives 19.14 to 19.72 / 31.73 to 32.28 %.
  • The direct route is the hardest to train. Attention over 4,200 tokens costs 20 times more per step than the multi-resolution model. With 20 epochs allowed on I2, validation was best at epoch 10 (25.0, against 24.0 to 24.7 for the cheaper models) and training stopped at epoch 15. On test it scores 19.56, 1.50 [+0.84, +2.35] points worse than the same model at 4 weeks and no better than at 1 week.

Recommendations

  1. For a trained 5-minute model, use 4 weeks of context. The multi-resolution transformer with bounded inputs, mean scaling, an L1 loss and all available history trains in about 7 minutes on one GPU. It beats the ladder by 1.3 (I2) and 4.5 points (Switch LAN) at a day.
  2. Bound the scaled input whenever context is long. Recent-mean scaling leaves old weeks unbounded, and the problem grows with context length.
  3. Do not expect 24 weeks to help a model trained from scratch on this data. Every approach tried tied 4 weeks at best. For day and week forecasts, Timestral with hourly input over 24 weeks remains the best per-link model; with 5-minute input it is still 1.0 to 1.7 points ahead of the best trained ensemble at a day.

Method and limits

  • Code: recipes/ladder_diagnosis/long5m.py (models, training, scoring), refs5m.py (reference scores, untrained profiles), compare.py (paired tests, now also on 5-minute WAPE), grids grids/l5*.txt. Scoring matches gr-dense metrics wape_1h, as in the earlier report.
  • History before the panels: Switch LAN 5-minute values from 2024-01 from the frozen S3 cache (hourly means identical to the earlier hourly file); I2 only hourly before 2025-11-15, so 64 % of I2's 24-week training contexts are hourly-filled while test contexts are all 5-minute. The hourly-summary variant controls for this and did not help.
  • Most runs are single seeds. Seeds exist for the 1, 4 and 24-week multi-resolution transformer. Two sets of runs were stopped early to free the GPU: the first full-resolution 24-week runs, which lacked bounded inputs, and the original grid's 12-week runs.
  • Timestral with 16 weeks of 5-minute input: 32,256 steps, near its 32,768 limit; 47 minutes on I2 and 2.7 hours on Switch LAN while sharing the GPU.
  • I2's test block is public and reaches Timestral's release date, so overlap with its training data cannot be ruled out. Switch LAN is private.
