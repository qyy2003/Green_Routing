Why the trained ladder trails Timestral

There is no bug, and the data are not the main limit. The ladder forecasts from one week of 5-minute history, and from that input every architecture, Timestral included, lands at about the same error. Timestral wins because it reads 24 weeks of hourly history and has learned, from a large pretraining corpus, how to use it. Three pipeline choices cost the ladder a further 1 to 5 points: 5-minute inputs and targets, log-space Huber training, and training on 7 months instead of all the history. Fixing them closes 57 % (I2) and 74 % (Switch LAN) of the gap. The trained models still gain little from long context.

Error against context length

Each point is a full test-block score. Timestral improves steadily as it sees more weeks. Trained models, even with 12 to 18 extra months of training history, stay nearly flat (at most 0.7 points from 1 to 4 weeks): they cannot turn more context into much better day-ahead forecasts.

Every architecture learns the same function

On the year run, DLinear (a linear map) reaches a validation loss within 1 % of PatchTST's (0.2105 vs 0.2089); TCN 0.2115, GRU 0.2171. On both networks the training loss stops falling after two or three epochs, with no sign of over-fitting. PatchTST's hourly errors correlate 0.91 to 0.95 with those of DLinear, GRU and TCN. When a linear model and a transformer agree this closely, the input sets the limit, not the model.

A week of history holds one sample of each hour of the week. Every model ends up blending last week's shape with the recent level. Timestral given the same week does the same and scores the same.

The low bias is a symptom

The ladder under-forecasts: median link bias at a day is −14 to −16 % on Switch LAN and −4.6 to −9.2 % on I2, against +0.4 % for the seasonal naive forecast. Modelling 5-minute values in log space aims at their geometric mean, and on Switch LAN the hourly arithmetic mean sits 6.4 % above the within-hour geometric mean for the median link (23.5 % at the 10th percentile; 0.8 % on I2). Rescaling every link to zero bias, even with the test block's own totals, does not lower WAPE: absolute error is minimised by the median, which lies below the mean for skewed traffic.

Can trained models use long context?

Not well. Feeding 4,032 raw hourly values into an MLP over-fits (I2 24.6 %, Switch LAN 32.5 %). Summaries work better: ProfMLP sees the last two weeks plus, for every hour of the week, the median over all K weeks and over the last four, and the weekly means. With extended history it is the best trained model at a week, but at a day it ties PatchTST given one week.

Where long context helps Timestral

On I2 the gain from 1 to 24 weeks is 3.1 to 3.7 points in every bucket of how unusual the last week was, from typical weeks to weeks more than 1.6 times off the median of weeks 2 to 8. It is a better estimate of level and shape everywhere, not only protection against an odd week. Against the ladder, both parts improve: daily-volume error 13.6 → 11.5 %, shape error at the true daily volume 15.6 → 12.9 %. Plain multi-week averages without learning reach only 22.8 % (I2) and 38.0 % (Switch LAN).

Recommendations

  1. Use Timestral for per-link day and week forecasts (24 weeks of hourly context). It is the best model on both networks, and on the private Switch LAN data it cannot have seen the test block.
  2. If a trained model is kept, rebuild it on hourly means for day and week horizons, with mean scaling and an L1 or pinball loss, trained on all history rather than the panel window, with weekly-profile features for long context. Keep 5-minute models for 15 minutes and 1 hour, where the ladder already ties Timestral (I2 1 h: TCN 9.45 vs 9.36 %).
  3. Compare models on equal information. The original comparison mixed "1 week vs 24 weeks" with "trained vs pretrained". Match context and resolution before ranking architectures.

Method and limits

  • The evaluator reproduces gr-dense metrics wape_1h exactly on the existing block logs (I2 day: PatchTST 19.146, Timestral 16.224, seasonal naive week 28.210).
  • All ablations ran in recipes/ladder_diagnosis/hourly_train.py, not the ladder's own code. Model selection used the validation block only. Each training run took seconds to about 25 minutes on one shared GPU.
  • Extra history: I2's hourly link file from 2024-11 matches the 5-minute panel within ±0.3 %; Switch LAN hourly 2024-01 → 2025-07 from the frozen S3 cache. Before 2025-02 the Switch LAN door set differs; per link it was used as is.
  • I2's test block is public and reaches Timestral's release date (2026-10-15), so overlap with its training data cannot be ruled out. Switch LAN is private and shows the same pattern.
  • Not tried: LoRA or Switch LAN fine-tuning of Timestral, quantile outputs, calendar covariates per link, the ladder's own code fed hourly input.
